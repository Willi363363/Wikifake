# Track N — the article of the day

| | |
|---|---|
| **State** | 🔶 N.1 done — the table and the claim |
| **Branch** | one per step |
| **Depends on** | tracks E and G — it stores a round and ranks it |
| **Delivers** | one article a day, the same for everybody, with its own board |

## Why

Every other track makes the game better for the player who is already playing.
This one gives a reason to **open the page at all**, on a day nobody planned to.

The ingredient every daily ritual has and this game lacks is **a shared subject**.
Today two players who both play at nine in the morning play different articles,
so there is nothing to say to each other and nothing to compare: a score is a
number about a round nobody else took. One article a day turns the same score
into a position.

**And it costs no content.** Wikipedia has six million articles in French; the
variety is already written, by somebody else, for ever. That is what makes this
compatible with the constraint that shaped track F — *once the app is launched I
want nothing left to do* — where a "daily challenge" of hand-picked themes would
not be.

## The four decisions this track has to make

### One article means one falsification, not one topic

Two players given the topic *Tour Eiffel* do not play the same round: the model
falsifies afresh and the sentences differ. **So the day's article is a generated
artefact and it is stored**, paragraphs and solution together — the same shape
`game` already keeps, because it is the same thing.

The Redis cache is not that store. It holds three variants per category for six
hours (`cache/keys.ts`), which is the opposite of what is wanted here: one
variant, for one day, the same for everybody.

### The topic is chosen by Wikipedia, not by a model

`sourceArticle` takes a topic; **nothing in this repository chooses one.** The
`topic_choice` kind exists in `llm_call` and is inherited from the Python; no
TypeScript caller produces it.

Asking a model daily would be a call that costs money, can fail, and needs
watching — three things the constraint above refuses. **MediaWiki's own random
endpoint** costs nothing, cannot invent an article that does not exist, and is
varied by construction. `list=random` with `rnnamespace=0`, beside
`searchTitles` and through the same transport.

Which is N.2, with the filtering a random article needs: an article with three
usable paragraphs, in a namespace that is an article at all.

### Determinism is storage, not a seed

Track F's generator is deterministic from the date, and that is **not** the shape
here. A seed reproduces a *choice*; it cannot reproduce a model's output, so two
players would still read different sentences from the same seeded topic.

So the day's article is decided once and written down, and everybody after reads
the row. Which makes the concurrency the interesting part.

### Who generates, when nobody has yet

F.5's rule holds and is inherited: **the cron is an optimisation, the read path
is the guarantee.** A day whose cron never ran must still have an article the
moment somebody asks.

But generation costs a model call, and fifty players arriving at midnight must
not buy fifty articles. **So the row is claimed before it is filled**, in two
phases:

```
insert into daily_article (day) on conflict (day) do nothing returning day
```

The caller that gets a row has the claim and generates. Every other caller gets
nothing back, reads the row, and finds it either finished or still empty — and
an empty one is *being generated by somebody else*, which is a wait rather than a
failure, and never a second call.

That is N.1, and it is why the table's article columns are nullable: **a row with
no article is not a broken row, it is a claim.**

## Steps

| # | Step | State |
|---|---|---|
| N.1 | The table, and the claim that wins | ✅ |
| N.2 | A topic chosen by Wikipedia, not by a model | ⬜ |
| N.3 | The read path and the cron | ⬜ |
| N.4 | A round on the day's article | ⬜ |
| N.5 | The day's board | ⬜ |
| N.6 | The entry point on the home dashboard | ⬜ |

### N.1 — the table

Keyed by `periodIndexOf('daily')`, the **same calendar as the quests**. Not a
`date` column: a date needs a timezone to mean a day, quests already decided that
question, and two calendars in one product is one of them being wrong somewhere.

The article columns are nullable, which is the claim above. `claimed_at` is not:
a row exists because somebody claimed it, and when is what says whether a claim
has been abandoned — a generation that crashed leaves a row nobody will ever
fill, and N.3 needs to be able to see that rather than serve an empty day for
ever.

## Exit gate

- Two callers claiming the same day concurrently: one generates, one reads.
- A day whose cron never ran still has an article for its first player.
- Two players on the same day are graded on the same sentences.
- The board for a day ranks only rounds played on that day's article.
